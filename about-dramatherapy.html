Therapeutic Approach

What is Dramatherapy?

Dramatherapy is a form of psychological therapy that uses creativity, imagination, play and expression to help us understand ourselves and our experiences.

Although it is called drama therapy, you do not need to be an actor, enjoy performing or have any artistic skills. There is no expectation to act, perform in front of anyone or create something that looks a particular way.

At its heart, dramatherapy is about finding different ways to explore what is happening in our lives, how we feel and how we relate to ourselves and others.

Sometimes, talking about our experiences can feel difficult. We might struggle to find the right words, feel overwhelmed by our emotions, or find ourselves repeating the same thoughts without quite understanding what lies underneath them.

Dramatherapy offers another way of exploring these experiences. Through imagination, images, objects, stories and creative activities, we can begin to express things that may be difficult to communicate through conversation alone.

A Holistic Approach to Healing

Unlike traditional talk therapy, dramatherapy engages the whole person – mind, body, and spirit – creating opportunities for profound healing and self-discovery. Through the safe medium of creative play and storytelling, individuals can explore difficult emotions and develop greater self-awareness.

This innovative therapeutic approach allows clients to safely explore their emotions, experiences, and relationships through techniques such as role-play, improvisation, storytelling, and creative movement. The therapeutic process is conducted by trained and licensed professionals who work collaboratively with clients to create a safe and supportive environment.

Evidence-Based Practice

Dramatherapy is recognized by the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) and is used in hospitals, schools, community centers, and private practice settings worldwide.

Therapeutic Techniques

Role-Play

Exploring perspectives and trying on different life roles safely.

Storytelling

Processing personal experiences and meaning through narrative.

Body Movement

Integrating physical expression with emotional awareness.

Creative Writing

Externalizing thoughts, feelings, and inner dialogues safely.

Sand Play & Drawing

Visualizing internal worlds and symbolic representations.

Metaphor & Improvisation

Building spontaneity, flexibility, and psychological insight.

Who Can Benefit?

Dramatherapy supports individuals navigating a diverse range of psychological and emotional challenges.

✓ Addiction recovery support
✓ Anxiety and depression management
✓ ADHD and autism spectrum support
✓ Bereavement and grief processing
✓ Cognitive impairments and dementia care
✓ Communication disorder assistance
✓ Eating disorder treatment
✓ Learning disability support
✓ Life transition guidance
✓ Personality disorder therapy
✓ Trauma and PTSD healing

Professional Recognition

Dramatherapy is a regulated profession recognized by professional bodies including the British Association of Dramatherapists (BADth) and the North American Drama Therapy Association (NADTA).
