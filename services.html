Specialized Treatment for Eating Disorders

A comprehensive, recovery-focused approach combining three complementary therapeutic approaches. Our approach is not centred on weight loss, but on supporting individuals to develop a healthier and more compassionate relationship with food, their bodies and themselves. Through structured support, therapeutic exploration and practical strategies, we aim to address the emotional, psychological and behavioural difficulties associated with eating disorders.

Dramatherapy for Eating Disorders

Flexible, Individualised Duration

Uses creativity, imagination, movement, storytelling, role-play and symbolic expression to explore emotions and experiences that may be difficult to put into words. It can help individuals develop emotional awareness, strengthen self-understanding, explore body image and identity, and discover alternative ways of relating to themselves and others.

CBT-T (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy- Ten)

Structured Evidence-Informed

A structured, evidence-informed approach that helps individuals understand and address the thoughts, beliefs, feelings and behaviours that contribute to and maintain eating difficulties. Sessions explore and gently challenge unhelpful beliefs and thinking patterns around food, weight, body image and self-worth. The approach focuses on establishing regular eating patterns and developing healthier coping strategies. Between-session activities or home tasks are usually encouraged to help individuals practise new skills and apply what they have learned in everyday life.

GSH (Guided Self-Help)

Usually 10–12 Sessions

A structured, supported approach using evidence-based self-help materials alongside regular therapeutic guidance and encouragement. It helps individuals understand their eating difficulties, work towards consistent eating patterns, monitor their progress and make manageable changes at their own pace.

Virtual Reality (VR) in Therapeutic Practice

We are interested in exploring how immersive technology can complement psychological and creative therapeutic approaches.

Where appropriate, guided virtual reality (VR) experiences may be incorporated into sessions as an optional, therapist-facilitated resource. This may be considered alongside different approaches, including dramatherapy, Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for Eating Disorders (CBT-T) and Guided Self-Help (GSH).

Depending on individual needs and therapeutic goals, VR may offer additional opportunities to explore sensory awareness, grounding, relaxation and emotional regulation.

Its use is discussed with each client and considered on an individual basis, taking into account personal preferences, suitability and comfort. VR is not a standalone treatment and does not replace the therapeutic relationship or the established therapeutic approach.

Integrated Treatment Approach
